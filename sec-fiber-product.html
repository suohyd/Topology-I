    Section 2.4 Pullback/Fiber product

    In this section we describe the pullback (also called fiber product) construction for continuous functions on topological spaces.

    Definition 2.4.1.

    Let \(X,Y\) and \(Z\) be topological spaces. Suppose that \(f\colon X\to Z\) and \(g\colon Y\to Z\) be continuous maps. The pullback of \(X,Y\) along continuous functions \(f,g\) is a topological space \(X\times_ZY\) together with continuous functions \(\pi_X\colon X\times_ZY\to X\) and \(\pi_Y\colon X\times_ZY\to Y\) such that \(f\circ\pi_X=g\circ\pi_Y\) and that satisfy the following universal property.
    If \(W\) is any topological space with continuous maps \(p_X\colon W\to X\) and \(p_Y\colon W\to Y\) such that \(f\circ p_X=g\circ p_Y\text{,}\) then there exists a unique continuous map \(h\colon W\to X\times_ZY\) such that \(p_X=\pi_X\circ X\) and \(p_Y=\pi_Y\circ h\text{.}\)

    Remark 2.4.3.

    We keep notations of Definition 2.4.1. Consider the following subset of \(X\times Y\) with the subspace topology.
    \begin{equation*} X\times_ZY=\left\{(x,y)\in X\times Y:f(x)=g(y)\right\} \end{equation*}
    It can be verified that the above subspace together with natural projection \(\pi_X\colon X\times_ZY\to X\) (resp., \(\pi_Y\colon X\times_ZY\to Y\)) given by \(\pi_X((x,y))=x\) (resp., given by \(\pi_Y((x,y))=y\)) is pullback.
    This shows existence of pullback.

    Remark 2.4.4.

    The pullback given in Remark 2.4.3 is unique up to homeomorphism.

    Example 2.4.5.

    Let \(X,Y\) be a topological spaces and let \(Z=\{\star\}\) be a one point set.
    Let \(f\colon X\to\{\star\}\) and \(g\colon Y\to\{\star\}\) be unique continuous maps. In this case we get that \(X\times_ZY\) is homeomorphic to \(X\times Y\text{.}\)
    Suppose that \(h\colon X\to Y\) is a continuous map. Let \(g'\colon\{\star\}\to Y\) be a map that maps \(g'(\star)=y_0\) for some \(y_0\in Y\text{.}\) We get that \(X\times_YZ\) is homeomorphic to \(h^{-1}(y_0)\text{.}\)

    Example 2.4.6.

    Suppose that \(Z\) is a topological space and that \(X,Y\) be subspaces of \(Z\text{.}\) Consider the canonical inclusions, viz., \(i_X\colon X\hookrightarrow Z\) and \(i_Y\colon Y\hookrightarrow Z\text{.}\) The pullback of \(X, Y\) along these inclusions \(i_X,i_Y\) is the following.
    \begin{align*} X\times_ZY \amp=\left\{(x,y)\in X\times Y:i_X(x)=i_Y(y)\right\} \\ \amp=\left\{(x,y)\in X\times Y:x=y\right\} \\ \amp\simeq X\cap Y \end{align*}

    Example 2.4.7.

    The continuous functions involved in the pullback construction are important. For instance the pullback of \(\R\) along identity functions is \(\R\times_\R\R=\{(x,x)\in\R\times\R\}\simeq\R\text{.}\) However, the pullback of \(\R\) along \(f\colon\R\to\R\) given by \(x\mapsto x^2\) along with the identity is \(\R\times_\R\R=\{(x,y)\in\R\times\R:x^2=y\}\text{.}\)
