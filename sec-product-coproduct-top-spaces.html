    Section 2.1 Product and coproduct topology

    In this section we define product and coproduct of topological spaces. We begin with the following proposition.

    Proposition 2.1.1.

    Let \(I\) be a nonempty indexing set, and \((X_i,\tau_i)\) be topological spaces for each \(i\in I\text{.}\) We consider a product of sets, \(X=\prod X_i\) as in Lemma 1.1.3. Following topologies on \(X\) are all equivalent.
    1. The topology \(\tau_1\) on \(X\) generated by the following basis.
      \begin{equation*} \left\{\prod U_i:U_i\subseteq X_i\text{open, and all but finitely many } U_i=X_i\right\} \end{equation*}
    2. The coarsest topology \(\tau_2\) on \(X\) that make all projection maps \(\pi_i\colon X\to X_i\) continuous.
    3. (Universal property for a product topology). Let \(\tau_3\) denote the topology on \(X\) having the following universal property.
      For every topological space \(Y\) and every map \(f\colon Y\to X\text{,}\) \(f\) is continuous if and only if for each \(i\in I\text{,}\) \(\pi_i\circ f\colon Y\to X_i\) is continuous; see the following diagram.

    Proof.

    \((1)\Leftrightarrow (2)\text{.}\) Suppose that \(U_i\subseteq X_i\) is an open set. Then, \(\pi_i^{-1}(U_i)\) is a product of sets whose \(j\)-th component is \(X_j\) when \(j\neq i\) and the \(i\)-th component is \(U_i\text{.}\) Therefore, any topology on \(X\) which makes \(\pi_i\) continuous, we must have \(\pi_i^{-1}((U_i))\) open in that topology, in particular, \(\pi_i^{-1}(U_i)\in\tau_2\text{.}\) Furthermore, given a basis element \(\prod U_i\in\tau_1\) with \(U_j=X_j\) for any \(j\not\in\{i_1,\ldots,i_n\}\text{,}\) we can write it as \(\bigcap_{k=1}^{n}\pi_{i_k}^{-1}(U_{i_k})\in\tau_2\text{.}\) Hence, \(\tau_1\) and \(\tau_2\) are equivalent.
    \((2)\Leftrightarrow (3)\text{.}\) Suppose that \(X\) has topology \(\tau_3\text{.}\) Consider the identity map \({1}_X\colon X\to X\) with the topology \(\tau_3\) on both the domain and the codomain. Then, given an open subset \(U_i\subseteq X_i\text{,}\) \(\pi_i^{-1}(U_i)=(\pi_i\circ {1}_X)^{-1}(U_i)\) is open in \(X\) because \({1}_X\) is continuous. Hence, \(\tau_2\subseteq\tau_3\text{.}\)
    Conversely, consider \(\unit_X\colon (X,\tau_2)\to(X,\tau_3)\) the identity map. We have the following diagram for each \(i\in I\text{.}\) By the definining property of \(\tau_2\text{,}\) for each \(i\in I\text{,}\) the map \(\pi_i\circ \unit_X\) is continuous. Therefore, by the defining property of \(\tau_3\text{,}\) the identity map \(\unit_X\colon (X,\tau_2)\to (X,\tau_3)\) is also continuous, i.e., an open subset of \(X\) with respect to \(\tau_3\) is open with respect to \(\tau_2\) as well. In particular, \(\tau_3\subseteq\tau_2\text{.}\)

    Definition 2.1.4. (Product of topological spaces).

    A product of topological spaces \((X_\alpha,\tau_\alpha)\) is set \(\prod X_\alpha\) with the topology given by any one of the topologies in Proposition 2.1.1.

    Remark 2.1.5.

    Let \(X,Y\) be topological spaces. The projection maps \(\pi_X\colon X\times Y\to X\) and \(\pi_Y\colon X\times Y\to Y\) are open maps. Furthermore, for any \((x,y)\in X\times Y\text{,}\) maps \(\pi_X\colon X\times\{y\}\to X\) and \(\pi_Y\colon \{x\}\times Y\to Y\) are homeomorphisms.
    The basic ingredient to see the above statement is the following observation.
    For \(U\subseteq X\) open and \(V\subseteq Y\) open
    \begin{equation*} \left(U\times V\right)\cap\left(X\times\{y\}\right)=U\times\{y\}\text{ if }y\in V\text{ and }\emptyset\text{ otherwise.} \end{equation*}
    We now consider the “coproduct topology”. We begin with the following proposition, which is analogues to Proposition 2.1.1. We leave the proof of the proposition as an exercise.

    Proposition 2.1.6.

    Let \(I\) be a nonempty set, and let \((X_\alpha,\tau_\alpha)\) be a topological space for each \(\alpha\in I\text{.}\) The following topologies on \(\coprod X_\alpha\) (see Definition 1.1.7) are equivalent.
    1. The topology \(\tau_1\) such that \(U\subseteq\coprod X_\alpha\) is open if and only if \(U=\coprod U_\alpha\) for some \(U_\alpha\subseteq X_\alpha\) open.
    2. The topology \(\tau_2\) such that it is the finest topology on \(\coprod X_\alpha\) such that inclusion maps \(i_\alpha\colon X_\alpha\to\coprod X_\alpha\) are continuous.
    3. (Universal property of a coproduct topology). The topology \(\tau_3\) satisfying the following universal property.
      For every topological space \(Z\) and every function \(f\colon\coprod X_\alpha\to Z\text{,}\) \(f\) is continuous if and only if for each \(\alpha\in I\text{,}\) \(f\circ i_\alpha\colon X_\alpha\to Z\) is continuous, where \(i_\alpha\colon X_\alpha\to\coprod X_\alpha\) is natural inclusion.

    Definition 2.1.8. (Coproduct of topological spaces).

    A coproduct of topological spaces \((X_\alpha,\tau_\alpha)\) is set \(\coprod X_\alpha\) with the topology given by any one of the topologies in Proposition 2.1.6.

    Example 2.1.9. (Product and coproduct of finitely many topological spaces).

    By Example 1.1.11, we may assume that \(\prod_{i=1}^{n}X_i=\coprod_{i=1}^nX_i\text{.}\) The product topology on \(\prod_{i=1}^nX_i\) and the coproduct topology \(\coprod_{i=1}^nX_i\) are the same.

    Checkpoint 2.1.10.

    We denote by \(A^t\) the transpose of \(A\in M_2(\R)\text{.}\) Show that the following map is continuous.
    \begin{equation*} M_2(\R)\to M_2(\R)\times\R\quad\text{given by}\quad A\mapsto\left(AA^t,\det A\right) \end{equation*}
    Find the preimage of the point \(\left(I_2,1\right)\text{.}\)
