    Section 2.3 Topological groups

    In this section we define and give some examples of topological groups.

    Definition 2.3.1.

    Let \(G\) be a group and a topological space. We say that \(G\) is a topological group if the group operation \(G\times G\to G\text{,}\) given by \((g,h)\mapsto gh\) and the inverse map \(G\to G\) given by \(g\mapsto g^{-1}\) are continous.

    Example 2.3.2. (Group of invertible matrices as a topological space).

    We consider \(\GL_n(\R)=\{A\in M_n(\R):\det A\neq 0\}\) as a subset of \(\R^{n^2}\text{,}\) and we give subspace topology on \(\GL_n(\R)\text{.}\) As the entries of product of matrices \(A,B\in \GL_n(\R)\) depend continuously on entries of \(A\) and \(B\text{,}\) the matrix multiplication map \(\GL_n(\R)\times \GL_n(\R)\to \GL_n(\R)\) is continuous. Furthermore, the entries of the inverse of \(A\) depends continuously on the entries of \(A\text{.}\) Hence, the inverse map \(\GL_n(\R)\to \GL_n(\R)\) given by \(A\mapsto A^{-1}\) is continuous.

    Example 2.3.3. (Special linear and special orthogonal groups).

    Similar to Example 2.3.2, we get that the following subgroups of \(\GL_n(\R)\) are topological groups.
    \begin{equation*} \SL_n(\R)=\left\{A\in M_n(\R):\det A=1\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\SO_n(\R)=\left\{A\in \SL_n(\R):AA^t=I_n\right\} \end{equation*}

    Checkpoint 2.3.4. (Equivalent condition).

    Show that the two conditions in Definition 2.3.1 is equivalent to the following.
    \begin{equation*} \text{The mapping }(g,h)\mapsto gh^{-1} \text{ from }G\times G\to G \text{ is continuous}. \end{equation*}

    Checkpoint 2.3.5. (Translations).

    Let \(G\) be a topological group. Fix \(g_0\in G\text{.}\) Show that mapping \(\ell_{g_0}\colon G\to G\) given by \(g\mapsto g_0g\) and \(r_{g_0}\colon G\to G\) given by \(g\mapsto gg_0\) are homeomorphisms.

    Checkpoint 2.3.6. (Additive group of the Euclidean space).

    Show that \(+\colon\R^n\times\R^n\to\R^n\) given by
    \begin{equation*} \left((x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n),(y_1,y_2,\ldots,y_n)\right)\mapsto\left(x_1+y_1,x_2+y_2,\ldots,x_n+y_n\right) \end{equation*}
    is continuous.
