    Section 2.2 Subspace topology

    Proposition 2.2.1.

    Let \((X,\tau)\) a topological space and let \(Y\) be a subset of \(X\text{.}\) Following topologies defined on \(Y\) are all equivalent.
    1. The topology \(\tau_1=\{U\cap X:U\text{ an open subset of }X\}\text{.}\)
    2. The coarsest topology \(\tau_2\) on \(Y\) for which the canonical inclusion \(i\colon Y\hookrightarrow X\) is continuous.
    3. (Universal property of subspace topology). The topology \(\tau_3\) on \(Y\) satisfying the following property.
      For every topological space \((Z,\tau_Z)\) and any function \(f\colon Z\to Y\text{,}\) \(f\) is continuous if and only if \(i\circ f\colon Z\to X\) is continuous. See the following diagram.

    Proof.

    The equivalence of topologies \(\tau_1\) and \(\tau_2\) is left as an exercise.
    We show that \(\tau_3\subseteq\tau_1\text{.}\) Consider the following diagram, where \(1_Y\colon Y\to Y\) is the identity map. Note that \(i=i\circ 1_Y\text{,}\) and with the topology \(\tau_1\) the inclusion \(i\colon (Y,\tau_1)\to X\) is continuous. By the universal property of \(\tau_3\text{,}\) the identity \(1_Y\colon(Y,\tau_1)\to (Y,\tau_3)\) is also continuous. Hence, \(\tau_3\subseteq\tau_1\text{.}\)
    We now consider the following. As \(1_Y\colon (Y,\tau_3)\to (Y,\tau_3)\) is continuous, using the universal property of \(\tau_3\text{,}\) we get that \(i=i\circ 1_Y\) is also continuous. Since the \(\tau_1\) is the coarsest topology that makes inclusion continuous, we have that \(\tau_1\subseteq\tau_3\text{.}\)

    Definition 2.2.5.

    Let \((X,\tau)\) be a topological space, and let \(Y\) be a subset of \(X\text{.}\) The subspace topology on \(Y\) is any of the topologies defined by Proposition 2.2.1.

    Checkpoint 2.2.6.

    Suppose that \(Y_\alpha\subseteq X_\alpha\) and that each \(Y_\alpha\) is given the subspace topology. We can consider a product topology on \(\prod Y_\alpha\) as well as subspace topology induced by a product topology on \(\prod X_\alpha\text{.}\) Are these topologies same on \(\prod Y_\alpha\text{?}\)
    Hint.
    We denote by \(\tau_p\) the product topology on \(\prod Y_\alpha\) and by \(\tau_s\) the subspace topology on \(\prod Y_\alpha\text{.}\)
    For any \(\alpha\text{,}\) the projection map \(\pi_\alpha\colon(\prod Y_\alpha,\tau_s)\to Y_\alpha\) is continuous. Indeed, for an open subset \(V_\alpha\subseteq Y_\alpha\text{,}\) \(\pi_\alpha^{-1}\left(V_\alpha\right)=\left\{(y_\beta):y_\beta\in V_\alpha\text{ when }\beta=\alpha\right\}\) is an open subset with respect to \(\tau_s\text{.}\) Thus, by the universal property of product topology \(1\colon\left(\prod Y_\alpha,\tau_s\right)\to\left(\prod Y_\alpha,\tau_p\right)\) is continuous, i.e., \(\tau_p\subseteq\tau_s\)

    Example 2.2.8. (Euclidean and product topology on \(\R^n\)).

    We consider \(\R\) with the usual topology. We show that the product topology on \(\R\times\R\times\cdots\times\R\) is same as the Euclidean topology on \(\R^n\text{.}\) Note that the projection maps \(\pi_i\colon\R^n\to\R\) are continuous. Therefore, using the universal property of the product topology we get that an open subset in the product topology is open in the Euclidean topology.
    A direct way to see this is following. Suppose that \(v=\left(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n\right)\in\R^n\text{.}\) Consider an open subset \(U=(x_1-\epsilon/\sqrt{n},x_1+\epsilon/\sqrt{n})\times\cdots\times(x_n-\epsilon/\sqrt{n},x_n+\epsilon/\sqrt{n})\) in the product topology. Note that \(B(v,\epsilon)\subset U\text{.}\) This shows that a basic open subset in the product topology is an union of open balls in the euclidean topology.
